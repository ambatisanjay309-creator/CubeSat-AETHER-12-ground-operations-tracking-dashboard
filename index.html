End of Mission

Deorbit & Regulatory Compliance

A satellite's design isn't finished until it accounts for how it leaves orbit. Regulatory deorbit windows have compressed sharply in the last few years, and Aether‑12's structure and deorbit system are built around the newer, stricter rule.

IADC Guideline
25 yrvoluntary
International guideline, adopted 2002 — not legally binding
FCC Rule (Design Target)
5 yrbinding
US federal regulation, effective Sept 2024 — applies to any US-licensed operator or foreign operator using US ground stations
ISO 24113
LEO‑scaled
Global engineering standard, updated 2023, referenced in most licensing regimes
Deorbit Compliance Reality
Industry Today
~50% success
Mega‑Constellation Target
95%+ success

Deorbit method: passive drag sail (InflateSail heritage — 10 m² sail on a 1 m inflatable mast). A square-pyramid sail geometry gives ~4× the drag area of a flat sail, cutting decay time from years to months. Our implementation stows the sail as four membrane quadrants (NE/NW/SE/SW) in a dedicated deployment cassette, released by burn-wire heater blocks on command. Combined with WE43 magnesium structural elements that fully ablate on re-entry, this gives Aether‑12 a zero ground-casualty profile under ESA DRAMA modeling.

Pointing & Stability

Attitude Determination & Control

Knowing where you are in orbit is only half the problem — the spacecraft also has to know which way it's pointing, and correct for drift, to keep solar panels sun-facing and the antenna aimed at the ground station. This is a full 3-axis stabilized system, not a passive or spin-stabilized design.

Attitude Sensing
Star Tracker
Paired with sun sensors and an IMU (inertial measurement unit) for continuous 3-axis orientation knowledge
Fine Pointing
Reaction Wheel
Provides precise, propellant-free torque for slewing and fine pointing control
Momentum Management
3‑Axis Magnetorquers
X/Y/Z magnetorquer coils desaturate the reaction wheel by pushing against Earth's magnetic field — no propellant required

This is why the Doppler and link-budget math above matters so much: without accurate attitude control, the antenna and solar wings can't stay reliably pointed, which is exactly the kind of failure mode the AI signal classifier and link-margin tracking above are designed to catch early.

Flight Computer

Onboard Radiation‑Tolerant Electronics

Commercial (COTS) computers are far cheaper than space-rated hardware, but need real protection to survive years in LEO. This is the specific mitigation strategy — not generic "shielding," but concrete, testable layers, built around our own OBC and Supervisor Module architecture.

COTS Reference Testing
BeagleBone Black
Proton-tested at 15.9 MeV in published research — ~0.5 single-event upset failures/year in LEO once shielded, used to validate our COTS mitigation strategy
Memory Protection
MR4A16BCMA35
Radiation-hardened MRAM with EDAC (error detection and correction) for critical data storage
Fault Recovery
TMS570 + TPL5010‑Q1
Radiation-tolerant microcontroller paired with an independent hardware watchdog timer for auto-reset on lockup
Onboard Architecture
OBC + Supervisor
Primary On-Board Computer module backed by an independent Supervisor Module that can detect and recover a hung OBC without ground intervention

Three-layer strategy: TID screening (component selection), SEL detection & recovery (watchdog), and memory integrity protection (EDAC). Combined with Z-graded shielding, this is what makes an unhardened commercial computer viable for a multi-year mission instead of the ~3-month lifespan COTS parts get with no mitigation at all.

Key Terms
AOS — Acquisition of SignalThe moment the satellite rises above the horizon and contact becomes possible.
LOS — Loss of SignalThe moment the satellite drops below the horizon and contact ends.
TCA — Time of Closest ApproachThe point of minimum distance / maximum elevation, and the strongest signal of the pass.
Free-Space Path LossSignal strength fades with the square of distance — this is why margin is worst near the horizon.
TID — Total Ionizing DoseCumulative radiation damage to electronics over the mission lifetime, measured in rad(Si).
South Atlantic AnomalyA region where Earth's magnetic field is weaker, allowing trapped radiation belt particles to dip closer to low orbit altitudes.
DemisabilityWhether a structure fully burns up on re-entry rather than surviving to impact the ground — a regulatory requirement, not just an engineering nicety.
SEU / SEL — Single Event Upset / Latch-upA bit flip or a stuck high-current state caused by a single high-energy particle strike, as opposed to gradual TID damage.
EDAC — Error Detection and CorrectionMemory hardware that can detect and automatically fix radiation-induced bit flips before they corrupt data.
MagnetorquerA coil that generates a magnetic field to push against Earth's own field, producing torque without using any propellant.
Reaction WheelA spinning flywheel that provides precise attitude control torque by conservation of angular momentum — the primary fine-pointing actuator.
Star TrackerAn optical sensor that identifies known star patterns to determine spacecraft orientation with high precision, independent of GPS.
